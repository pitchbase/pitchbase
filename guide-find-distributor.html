Buyer guide

How to find a China distributor for a foreign brand

A distributor is a long-term relationship, not a purchase. The work is in defining what you need, checking stated channels against reality, and agreeing terms in writing before you commit.

PitchBase Fields buyer guide · Last update 2026-10-29

Define what you need first

Write down territory, product category, target channels, and what you expect the distributor to handle — warehousing, sales, marketing, after-sales — before you shortlist anyone. A distributor who is strong in one channel and silent in another is only a fit if your plan matches their strength.

Shortlist from stated information

Use the Partners directory to shortlist by category and region, then read each entry's stated focus, brands, and channels. Treat every stated claim as a lead to check, not as a fact: confirm channels and brand relationships through the distributor's own public pages and, where possible, through the brands themselves.

Prepare a one-page brand brief

Before outreach, prepare one page: what the product is, who buys it, retail positioning, current markets, and what you need from a China partner. A clear brief gets serious replies; a vague enquiry gets vague replies, and the quality of replies is itself a filter.

Questions worth asking

  • Which channels do you currently sell through, and which brands in my category do you already carry?
  • Do you hold stock, or order against demand? Where is warehousing, and who handles after-sales?
  • What marketing do you run for a new brand, and who pays for it?
  • What minimums, payment terms, and reporting do you work to?
  • Can you name two brands you have launched in China, and what happened?

Put terms in writing

Territory, exclusivity, pricing, minimums, marketing commitments, reporting, trial period, and exit terms — all in writing before stock ships. Exclusivity without performance terms is the most common regret: if a distributor holds your territory and does nothing with it, you are stuck. A defined trial period with review criteria protects both sides.

Start with a trial period

Agree a limited territory or a limited time first, with clear review points. A distributor who performs in a trial has earned expansion; one who does not has cost you a trial, not your China business.
